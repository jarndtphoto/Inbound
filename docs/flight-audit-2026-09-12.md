# UA1532 and AA3008 audit — September 12, 2026

This is a bounded audit of the flight-story data path, not certification of live surface-position accuracy.

| Flight | Observation | Corrected behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UA1532, ORD–MSY | At 16:52 UTC the app showed a synthetic live position near Chicago, airborne=true and measured taxi-out. FlightAware supplied gate-out 16:43 UTC, an airborne status, no position fix, and estimated takeoff 17:03 UTC with no actual takeoff. | At 16:56 UTC the updated app retained actual gate-out, showed the push stage, airborne=false, live=false, and estimated/posted taxi duration. |
| AA3008, ORD–LAX | At 16:52 UTC no actual departure or live fix was supplied. On the later query, FlightAware supplied actual gate-out 16:56 UTC. | At 16:58 UTC the updated app registered the 11:56 AM CDT pushback, kept takeoff unconfirmed, and supplied no invented live position. |

The sanitized operational fixtures in `scripts/fixtures` replay the earlier provider records with a fixed clock. Two integration tests verify both flights' routes, the lack of synthetic fixes, pushback semantics, unmeasured taxi estimates, and bounded weather queries. A third three-step replay verifies that a stationary taxi hold near the stand does not erase observed pushback, and that fresh ground motion does not wait for a slower trace request. Another 35 focused fusion, weather, and FlightAware tests pass. `npm run build` and `npm run typecheck` pass.

Route weather now checks SIGMET/CWA validity intervals and the applicable G-AIRMET/TCF forecast period at each route sample's estimated arrival time. The timestamp field names and formats were checked against live Aviation Weather Center GeoJSON responses on September 12. This prevents an advisory that has expired, or a forecast for a different hour, from being counted solely because its polygon intersects the route. A TAF passenger summary now reads the forecast periods relevant to the flight time, without calling a later storm current or reusing an expired forecast. These validity windows are forecasts, not a guarantee of actual conditions. A fourth replay verifies that when flight and route providers return no route, the app reports the route as unavailable instead of inventing an ORD–LAX flight and briefing.

The dev server starts using an environment-only fallback for unavailable network-interface enumeration. The shared Vercel preview loaded in a cloud browser; UA1532 and AA3008 rendered and their routes, registrations, gate-out and takeoff times were inspected. On September 12, Flightradar24 listed UA1532 as ORD–MSY, N485UA, with takeoff 17:06 UTC and ETA 18:53 UTC; the app showed takeoff 17:07 and landing ETA 18:57. Flightradar24 listed AA3008 as ORD–LAX, N887NN, with takeoff 17:14 and ETA 21:04; the app showed takeoff 17:15 and landing ETA 21:02. Small source/time differences remain. The wider npm test suite previously failed in platform/PWA metadata tests; npm ci also reported a pre-existing lockfile mismatch. No dependency or runtime workaround was committed.

At about 18:31–18:35 UTC, the preview incorrectly assigned **the same United 787-10, N14019**, to Southwest WN2512 (MDW–LGB), WN298 (MDW–HOU), and WN534 (MDW–OMA); all appeared airborne with future push/takeoff times. The [FR24 MDW departures board](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/airports/mdw/departures) listed WN2512 as a B38M scheduled for 13:50 CDT, estimated takeoff 14:27, with no actual departure at the time. Its [flight history](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/flights/wn2512) listed N8961K. The root cause was an unverified fallback aircraft candidate, compounded by treating a future provider “actual” timestamp as already occurred. The follow-up patch restricts callsign, registration and hex searches to matching aircraft, rejects a conflicting assigned tail or a distant airborne target before scheduled takeoff, and ignores future actual timestamps. The WN2512 replay with a mismatched 787 and future actual times, plus the previous four replays and 35 focused tests, passed locally. The deployed preview still needs a repeat check after this commit is built.

Remaining limits: all three ADS-B providers timed out in the local environment; exact live surface coordinates and pushback/taxi detection latency are unverified. FlightAware's public surface map requires a paid subscription, so the available pages cannot independently establish gate departure at second-level resolution. The story's cold-load deadline has been raised from 12 to 20 seconds, but its live latency has not been verified. PIREP recency, advisory motion, forecast uncertainty, and the complete route/altitude/time validity of the weather briefing need further review. The PR remains draft pending real surface-transition validation.
